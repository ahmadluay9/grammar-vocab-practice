# Unit 3, Lesson 3
## Dealing with flight problems

### Expressions - Flight problems
#### Delays and cancellations
> It's important that you understand messages about flight problems whether you get them as text messages, emails or by going to the airline's website. Notice the structure of the sentences with **has + been**.

1. Flight EA 654 to New Delhi **has been delayed**. It now departs at 5 p.m.
2. Flight EA 762 to Cairo **has been canceled**.

#### Reasons for flight problems
> Airlines usually give a general reason for a flight delay or cancellation. In written messages, they often use the phrase **due to**; but when you're speaking to an agent, **because of** is more common.

1. Flight EA 762 to Cairo has been canceled **due to maintenance issues**.

2.  - A: Excuse me. What's going on with my flight?
    - B: I'm very sorry sir, but it has been canceled **because of a security threat**.

#### Explaining the situation
> When your flight has been canceled, you need to contact an airline representative, either at the airport or on your phone, and explain your situation. They will need your name, flight number and a description of the problem.

1. Hi. I'm Martina Lopez, and I was on Flight 745 to Houston, the one that was just canceled.


#### Rebooking a flight
> Some airlines will automatically rebook you on a subsequent flight. Don't hesitate to push for a better option.

1.  - A: We've rebooked you on the same flight tomorrow.
    - B: That won't work. I have to be there tomorrow morning.

2.  - A: OK. I have a couple of options that get you there tomorrow morning.
    - B: Great. What are they?

> Be sure to confirm the information before you end the conversation, and get the changed flight information sent to you in an email or as a new boarding pass.

### Reading - Travel tips
Tips for flying

If you fly anywhere these days, you have probably experienced the unfortunate situation of a canceled flight. Here are some tips on how to deal with this increasingly common airline experience.

Plan ahead. If possible, try to fly nonstop.

Fly early in the day. If your flight is canceled or delayed, you have more options for being rebooked on a later flight. If you are on one of the last flights of the day, you will be in trouble if that flight is canceled.

Pack lightly. If possible, just have carry-on baggage. It's much easier to reschedule a flight if you haven't checked bags.

Have your airline phone numbers stored on your cellphone. This is very important because the first thing you want to do when you hear your flight has been canceled is to call the airline immediately. Of course, everyone else will also be calling, so expect to be placed on hold. If your flight is canceled due to inclement weather, many flights may also be canceled, and the call center representatives will be flooded with calls. But if you are placed on hold, just wait patiently.

Get in line to rebook, as suggested by the airline, but stay online with the airline call center. Talk with whomever can take care of you first. Also, some airports have special kiosks for self-service rebooking.

Be polite to the ticket agent or call center agent. Your problem isn't the fault of that person. You can even say something like 'I know it's not your fault, but I'm not happy right now because …' Rude or demanding passengers will likely end up with less. This is a situation where politeness can make all of the difference.

Regulations about what airlines are required to offer customers vary widely. However, in general, if the situation is beyond the airline's control (weather, air-traffic delays), you probably won't be offered compensation or assistance. If the situation is within the airline's control (maintenance problem, lack of a flight crew), you probably will be given some compensation, like meal or hotel vouchers. Remember, though, it never hurts to ask for assistance, as long as you ask politely.
